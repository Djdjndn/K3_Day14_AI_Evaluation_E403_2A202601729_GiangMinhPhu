## 5. Regression Testing Strategy

**Question 1: When should `run_regression()` run in production?**

> Run it before every deployment, after prompt changes, retrieval/chunking changes, model changes, and updates to safety policies.

**Question 2: Is a 0.05 drop threshold appropriate for Student Services? Why?**

> It is a reasonable default for broad averages, but safety/privacy and Faithfulness should be stricter. A small average drop can hide one severe privacy or hallucination failure, so critical cases should also have per-case gates.

**Question 3: Which metrics/failures block deployment, and which only alert?**

> Block deployment for Faithfulness below threshold, privacy/safety failures, prompt-injection failures, and major drops in Context Recall for policy questions. Alert for moderate Relevance or Completeness drops when the answer remains safe and grounded.

**Question 4: Fill the evaluation stages into the flow.**

```text
Code/prompt/retrieval change ? Offline evaluation ? Regression check ? Human review for critical failures ? Deploy
```

> Offline evaluation catches repeatable failures, regression check compares against baseline, and human review handles high-stakes safety/privacy or ambiguous cases before deployment.

## 7. Final Reflection

**What in the benchmark result was surprising?**

> Retrieval performed very well overall, but the worst case A01 had zero retrieval because the medical query did not surface the scope document. Also, some safe answers scored poorly because the heuristic expected Northstar-specific refusal wording, not just a generic safe refusal.

**What are the limits of word-overlap heuristics, and what would I add in production?**

> Word overlap can underrate semantically correct paraphrases and safe refusals, while overrating answers that reuse keywords without fully satisfying policy intent. In production, I would add LLM-as-a-judge with a calibrated rubric, citation/grounding checks, adversarial safety tests, and human review for privacy or appeal-related cases.
